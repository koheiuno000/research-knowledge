## 2. Research Question

What does available evidence show about **how extreme weather events affect education outcomes**, through **direct** channels (access, closures, infrastructure, school environment) and **indirect** channels (household economic shocks, health, nutrition, conflict, displacement), and what are **implications for adaptation** and **evidence gaps**?

## 4. Conceptual Focus

- **Figure 1 framework:** climate change → extreme weather → **proximate/direct** effects on education (learning, dropouts, long-term outcomes via access, closures, infrastructure, school environment) and **distal/indirect** effects (economic shocks, air quality, conflict, food insecurity, migration/displacement → readiness, demand, service disruption).
- **Four direct hazard channels** in detail: extreme heat; tropical cyclones/floods; droughts; wildfires.
- **Educational outcomes:** learning (short-run test scores / learning measures), enrollment, dropouts, longer-term attainment and related human-capital outcomes as reviewed in underlying studies.
- **Adaptation and mitigation** as urgent for education systems; education sector as part of resilience—not estimated as an intervention effect here.

## 5. Evidence base and review approach

**Data and sample:** **Secondary synthesis**—no original microdata. The review draws on **published empirical studies and selected prior reviews** on extreme weather and education (see Table 1 and hazard-specific sections), spanning multiple countries and hazards; not a PRISMA systematic map.

- Motivation: education underrepresented in climate-impact reviews (authors note **3 of 15** economic climate reviews since 2010 mention education; Web of Science imbalance vs health).
- Scope: **extreme weather** (heat, cyclones/floods, drought, wildfire), **direct and indirect** impacts, **short- and long-term** educational indicators.
- Organization: framework → direct channels by hazard → indirect channels → protecting/leveraging education → conclusions and gaps.
- **Table 1** (and related tables) summarize **key papers** included; this knowledge base treats those entries as **secondary bibliographic pointers** until each primary study is reviewed locally.

## 6. Direct pathways (proximate effects)

Authors define **direct/proximate** effects as impacts through **compromised school access**, **school closures**, **destruction of school structures**, and **impacts on school environment** (including heat in schools).

**Extreme heat**

- Evidence of **adverse heat–learning** relationship; exceeding temperature thresholds makes learning difficult.
- Pattern across studies: **additional days of extreme heat** associated with **reduced learning** (magnitudes from **cited primary studies**, not re-estimated here).

**Tropical cyclones / floods**

- Precipitate **closures** and infrastructure damage; disrupt attendance and schooling continuity.
- Recent event examples (e.g., cyclone-related closure statistics) illustrate scale; causal evidence comes from **underlying econometric studies** cited.

**Droughts**

- Reviewed links to enrollment, dropout, and human-capital-related outcomes via agricultural/income and water channels (direct and overlapping with indirect).

**Wildfires / air quality**

- Closures and **air quality** impacts on school operations and environment; learning and attendance disruptions.

**School closures and learning (direct disruption channel)**

- Abstract and introduction: evidence **suggests** roughly **one day of closure ↔ one day of learning lost** as a **synthesized pattern** from the literature—not a new estimate produced by this review.

---

## 7. Indirect pathways (distal effects)

**Distal/indirect** effects operate through **economic shocks**, **poor air quality**, **conflict and food insecurity**, **migration and displacement**, and related mechanisms that:

- disrupt education **services**,
- reduce **demand for schooling** (household coping),
- reduce student **readiness** (health and nutrition).

Authors tie these to **vulnerability** and **existing inequalities** (children, low-income regions, fragile settings).

| Drought | Enrollment, dropout, longer-term human capital | Negative impacts via income/agriculture/water stress (studies cited) |
| Wildfires | Closures, air quality | Disruption and environmental harm to learning conditions |

## 9. Educational outcomes covered

- **Learning** (short-run measures, e.g., test scores)
- **Enrollment and dropouts**
- **Longer-term outcomes** (attainment, human capital)—as present in reviewed primary literature
- Authors emphasize that pathways **ultimately** reduce learning and attainment, with economic consequences (cite Zivin and Shrader 2016 for human-capital/wealth framing)

## 10. Heterogeneity and vulnerable populations

- **Children** particularly at risk; UNICEF and related statistics on exposure to climate hazards.
- **Developing countries** and **within-country vulnerable communities** face greater burden with less evidence base (Sub-Saharan Africa, Southeast Asia noted).
- **Gender, poverty, fragility** discussed in connection with indirect channels and displacement (details in hazard-specific subsections and Table 1 studies).

---

## 11. Adaptation and policy implications

- **Adapt education systems** to climate change; **mitigation and adaptation** both urgent as extremes intensify.
- Addressing **underlying vulnerabilities** can reduce educational disparities and **increase resilience** to hazards.
- **Leverage education sector** for preparedness and protection (conceptual/policy; not causal program evaluation in this review).

---

## 12. Limitations and evidence gaps

- Education **under-studied** relative to health in climate economics.
- **Uneven geographic evidence** (most vulnerable regions often **least** covered).
- Review is **not exhaustive systematic** synthesis; **no pooled effect sizes**.
- **Mediation** (e.g., closure → learning) is discussed conceptually and via cited studies, **not** unified causal mediation analysis in this paper.
- **Adaptation interventions** lack a consolidated causal evidence base in the review.

**Next primary papers for this knowledge base (candidates, not reviewed here):** Baez, Caruso, and Niu (2020); David et al. (2018); Deuchert and Felfe (2015); Huang and Dong (2025); Palacios and Rojas-Velásquez (2023)—metadata may appear in this review’s tables but **require original-PDF verification** before inclusion as reviewed evidence.
