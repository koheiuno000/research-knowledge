# Grönqvist, Öckert & Rosenqvist (2025) — Does the “Boost for Mathematics” Boost Mathematics? (Sweden)

**Primary category:** `07_teacher-pd-interventions`

**Secondary themes:** Lesson study–style collaborative PD; teaching practice (teacher self-report); student mathematics achievement; national at-scale in-service training; high-income (Sweden) quasi-experimental evidence; staggered difference-in-differences

---

## Paper type and design (read first)

| Question | Answer |
|----------|--------|
| **What type of paper is this?** | **Large-scale quasi-experimental evaluation** of Sweden’s national **“Boost for Mathematics”** in-service program (Lesson study–style learning cycles with external tutors), exploiting **staggered rollout** across compulsory schools. |
| **Not** | A **cluster RCT** (contrast Loyalka 2019, Kozuka 2025). **Not** a cross-program synthesis (contrast Popova 2022). **Not** a meta-analysis with other studies in this database. |
| **Unit of analysis** | **Students** (national exam scores; expected-school assignment); **teachers** (panel survey at school level for mechanism outcomes). |
| **Identification** | **Stacked event-study / staggered difference-in-differences** (Sun & Abraham 2020; Callaway & Sant’Anna 2020); **never-treated schools only** as controls; school-level clustered SEs. **Reduced-form** effects of **expected school treatment** on student scores. |
| **What claims the design supports** | **Causal interpretation** of **PD rollout → student mathematics achievement** and **PD → teacher-reported collaboration/training/classroom time use**, under parallel-trends and exogeneity arguments—not **Teacher Knowledge → Teaching Practice**, **Teaching Practice → Student Achievement**, or **mediation** pathways. |

## 2. Research Question

Does a one-year national **Lesson study–style** mathematics PD program (the **Boost for Mathematics**) improve **student mathematics achievement**, and how does it change **teacher training, peer collaboration, and self-reported classroom practices**?

## 4. Teacher Knowledge (Constructs and Measurement)

**Not measured** with a content, MKT, or pedagogical knowledge test.

Table 5 and survey items on **in-service hours** (including hours involving “subject knowledge in mathematics”) measure **participation in training activities**, not knowledge gains. Appendix Table C3/C4 report **confidence** and beliefs about colleagues’ improvement—treated as attitudes/perceptions, not teacher knowledge outcomes for this database.

---

## 5. Teaching Practice (Constructs and Measurement)

**Measured:** Yes, as **mechanism outcomes** of PD—not as predictors of achievement.

**Method:** **Teacher questionnaire** (panel 2013–2016); teachers assigned to the school where they work (**ATT** interpretation in text).

**Dimensions:**
- **Peer collaboration** (frequency per term): joint planning, follow-up, didactic discussion, etc. (Table 6).
- **Classroom time use** (% of typical week): lecture, discuss problem-solving, students solving problems, tests, other activities (Table 7).

**Critical distinction:** These are **self-reported instructional time shares and collaboration frequency**, **not** independent **classroom observation** protocols (e.g., Kozuka’s 14-criterion observer checklist; Loyalka’s student-reported scales are also distinct).

**PD → practice:** Estimated via the same staggered DiD/event-study framework as training outcomes.

---

## 6. Student Outcomes

**Subject:** **Mathematics** (national centralized exams at grades 3, 6, 9).

**Scoring:** Test scores **standardized by year** in the full population (mean 0, SD 1 within year).

**Assignment:** Students sampled at **beginning** of stage (grades 1/4/6) and assigned **expected school’s** treatment status at **end** of stage (grades 3/6/9)—**reduced-form ITT** (~84% attend expected school; authors discuss scaling but primary estimate remains reduced-form).

**Primary pooled post-implementation effect:** **+0.0263 SD** (SE 0.0085, Table 2 Panel B).

**Heterogeneity:** Null/small for lowest quartile of predicted scores; positive for upper quartiles (Table 4). Primary stages (3 & 6) drive pooled effects; grade 9 estimate insignificant.

## 7. Data and Sample

- **Country:** Sweden (compulsory school).
- **Education level:** Grades **3, 6, 9** (lower/middle/higher stage exam endpoints).
- **Subject:** Mathematics.
- **Student analytic sample:** Up to **2,874,158** student observations (Table 2); **959–1,331** schools in descriptive Table 1 depending on participation wave.
- **Teacher survey:** ~**7,819–8,384** observations in mechanism tables (Tables 5–7).
- **Timeline:** Program waves **2013, 2014, 2015**; long-run follow-up up to **4–5 years** post-implementation for some cohorts.

## 8. Methods and Identification

| Element | Detail |
|--------|--------|
| Design | **Staggered rollout** quasi-experiment; **not randomized** assignment to Boost. |
| Estimation | Dynamic event study; stacked cohorts; **never-treated schools** as controls only (Sun & Abraham 2020; Callaway & Sant’Anna 2020). |
| Fixed effects | School×implementation wave; calendar time×voucher/municipal×wave. |
| Inference | Cluster-robust SEs at **school** level. |
| Student scores | **Reduced-form ITT** on expected school treatment. |
| Teacher surveys | DiD on teachers at treated schools (text: **ATT**). |

**Claim strength:** Authors argue **causal** effects of program rollout on achievement and teacher reports under parallel trends (pre-treatment coefficients near zero; predicted composition unchanged). **Distinct from RCT identification** in Loyalka/Kozuka. **Do not pool** effect sizes with those RCTs or compute a meta-analytic mean in this database.

## 9. Main Findings (Literature-Review Priorities)

### A. Empirical — PD → student mathematics achievement (staggered DiD, reduced-form)

- **Primary pooled post years:** **+0.0263 SD** (SE 0.0085, ***), Table 2 Panel B (all stages).
- **By stage (pooled post):** Grade 3 **+0.0447 SD***; grade 6 **+0.0286 SD**; grade 9 **+0.0044 SD** (n.s.).
- **Post windows (all stages):** 0–1 years **+0.0184**; 2–3 years **+0.0347**; 4–5 years **+0.0264** (Panel A).
- **Implementation year (τ = 0):** ~**+0.012 SD**, **p = 0.105** (not significant)—separate from pooled post estimate.

### B. Empirical — PD → teaching practice (self-report, staggered DiD)

- **Classroom time (Table 7, τ = 0):** More time on **problem-solving discussion** (+1.91 pp, **); less on **routine problem work** (−3.32 pp, ***); more **other activities** (+1.49 pp, **). Authors argue some shifts **persist** in τ = 1–2.
- **Peer collaboration (Table 6):** Higher collegial planning and didactic discussion during implementation year; most collaboration measures **fade** after implementation (unlike persistent time-use shifts).

### C. Training hours (not mapped to teacher knowledge)

- Table 5: Large increases in reported **in-service hours** (mathematics content, didactics, coaching, collaboration) during implementation year; largely **not maintained** in later years.

### D. Authors’ mechanism narrative (not causal pathway estimates)

Persistent **self-reported** instructional shifts are discussed as explaining **longer-run** test score gains; **no** formal link from practice measures to student scores.

## 11. Relevance to My Study

- **Complement to RCT base:** Adds **positive at-scale quasi-experimental** evidence for **Lesson study–style** national PD on **math achievement**, alongside **null China RCT** (Loyalka) and **mixed Ethiopia RCT** (Kozuka)—**compare designs separately**, do not meta-pool.
- **Measurement:** Highlights **self-reported practice** vs **observed practice** (Kozuka)—important when synthesizing “PD → practice.”
- **Policy scale:** Real-world staggered national rollout vs single-site or province RCTs.

**Do not cite for:** Causal **practice → achievement** or **knowledge → practice**; training hours as **knowledge gains**; **0.031 SD** as the primary ITT row (use **0.0263 SD** reduced-form).

## 12. Limitations (For My Use)

- **Version mismatch (quantitative):** Effect Summary uses the **IFAU 2021:22** PDF; journal (2025) not checked for identical tables or estimates.
- **Quasi-experiment:** School/district participation timing—not RCT; selection concerns mitigated but not eliminated.
- **Practice outcomes:** **Self-report** only; no independent observation.
- **No teacher knowledge test.**
- **Equity:** Weak/null effects for lowest predicted-score quartile.
- **External validity:** Sweden (HIC); differs from LMIC-focused papers in the same area.
- **Concurrent national reforms:** Partially controlled (Table 3 col. 3).

## Researcher Takeaway

**Core contribution:** Large-scale **staggered DiD** evaluation of Sweden’s **Boost for Mathematics** (Lesson study–style PD) finds **moderate positive reduced-form effects on national mathematics exam scores** (primary **+0.0263 SD**, SE 0.0085) and **changes in self-reported classroom time use and collaboration** during implementation—**without** teacher knowledge tests or **estimated** practice→achievement pathways.

**Best use in my literature review:** Folder **07** — quasi-experimental **PD → achievement** and **PD → self-reported practice** evidence at **national scale**, explicitly **not** an RCT and **not** pooled with Kozuka/Loyalka effect sizes.

**Priority:** **Core** — fills gap on **Lesson study–style** at-scale evaluation with achievement effects; contrasts with null UK Lesson study RCT cited in introduction.
